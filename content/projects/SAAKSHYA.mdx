## The problem

Organisations running physical projects — a lake clean-up, a plantation — collect photos across sites and
reporting periods. Turning those photos into a claim someone else can trust is the hard part: the
before and after are shot from different places, the numbers in the report are written by the people
being assessed, and there is no independent voice.

## What SAAKSHYA does

SAAKSHYA (Sanskrit and Hindi for *evidence* and *testimony*) puts that evidence into a ledger so
reviewers can compare site images, inspect integrity signals, accept independent witness submissions,
and check report statements against cited evidence.

- **Upload and inspect.** Images go straight from the browser to Cloudinary using server-signed
  parameters. SAAKSHYA reads the metadata and analysis fields that are available, and records anything
  missing as missing rather than guessing.
- **Register and measure.** A Python and OpenCV worker aligns each follow-up image to a site's baseline
  and returns registration quality, change metrics, an aligned image and a difference image. Jev, a
  typed decision model from TypeSafe AI, makes decisions from those measurements; it does not look at
  pixels.
- **Review witness evidence.** A public site link accepts submissions from passers-by without an
  account, and that evidence enters the same pipeline to be reviewed beside the project's own.
- **Check reports.** The report composer drafts from ledger facts and asks Jev to check each sentence
  against the facts it cites. The internal view shows which sentences were kept, struck or still
  pending, with reasons; the public view shows the kept ones.

## The rule it is built around

*Measure, then decide, then write.* Numbers come from deterministic code, judgements from Jev, and prose
is checked against the evidence ledger. Claims carry a receipt, uncertainty is shown, and when a service
is unavailable the decision is recorded as **pending** instead of fabricated.

## Honest limits

The repository states these itself, so the case study does too. Cloudinary does signed upload, storage,
selected metadata, transformations and structured search; it does not do the registration or change
measurement. Its captioning, auto-tagging and advanced OCR add-ons were recorded as unavailable when
last probed, and C2PA signing and Cloudinary-side difference analysis are not current capabilities. The
judge walkthrough's images are seeded repository fixtures, not field evidence — and so is the banner
above, a baseline and follow-up pair from the repository's test images.

## Stack

Next.js 15 with React 19 and TypeScript for the web app, a Python FastAPI and OpenCV worker for image
registration, a PostgreSQL-compatible ledger (PGlite locally, Neon or Supabase in production), and
Groq-backed drafting for report prose. A live deployment and a judge walkthrough are linked from the
repository.
