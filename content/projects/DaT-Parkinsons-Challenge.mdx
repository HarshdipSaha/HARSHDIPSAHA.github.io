## The task

DrivenData Competition #311 asks one question of each 3D DaT-SPECT volume: normal or abnormal? The metric
is log loss, so confident wrong answers are punished hard, and the entry is a code-execution
`submission.zip` that runs on the organiser's hardware rather than a file of predictions.

## Result

The competition closed on September 16, 2026. The official private leaderboard placed this entry at
**rank #50**, with a best private log loss of **0.3003** and an AUROC of **0.9406** (submission
`id-322091`); its best public log loss was **0.2473**. All 6 official submissions and 20 smoke tests are
audited in the repository.

The point of publishing it is less the rank than the record. The repository keeps every score next to
the submission id that produced it, a compliance and disclosure note, a field guide written from the
run, and a deep retrospective on what went wrong.

## What it learned

The field guide's central observation is about where the gains came from: the first big improvement
came from fixing the pipeline, the next from ensembling, and the last from arithmetic on cached
predictions. A few of its rules, each earned by a specific failure:

- **Every number needs a provenance.** A session once wrote a score into the log that had never happened.
  A score is data only if you can name the submission id, timestamp and configuration it came from.
- **Check that a claimed gain is arithmetically possible.** One change appeared to improve the score by
  more than the largest effect its supposed cause could have had, so the cause was something else.
- **Validation without shift cannot price robustness.** Random-fold cross-validation has no distribution
  shift in it, so it always prefers sharper, less regularised predictions. Hold out something that
  resembles the shift you will face — here, the hospital site — and make robustness decisions there.

The figure above is from the accompanying write-up: log loss under random-fold validation against
leave-one-site-out validation, across four model families. The radiomics models look better under random
folds than they are across sites; the 3D CNNs hold steady.

## Repository

Notebooks for each stage (EDA, scanner shift, registration, radiomics, a model zoo, the final ensemble),
the shared package, cache-building and packing scripts, and the architecture decisions behind them. The
work was run under an ML-specific AI-DLC protocol, with nine documented efforts.
