## Status

**Winner, AI Arena 3.0.** Built with Anshika Singh.

## The problem

People submit images as proof: a receipt for a refund, a bill for a claim. Every AI document-fraud
detector answers *"is this fake?"* with a confidence score you cannot check. If it is wrong, nothing
in its answer shows you why.

## How it works

VERDICT answers differently. A team of agents must **spend a budget on deterministic forensic probes**
to earn evidence: error-level analysis, JPEG quantization tables, noise residuals, copy-move,
typography. Every claim must **cite** the exact crop it came from, by content hash. The probes are
decades-old forensics; what is new is the architecture around them.

- **Perceptual starvation.** The agent with the authority to decide never sees the image. It reasons
  only over a text manifest of evidence cards that another agent had to pay to acquire, so it cannot
  answer without looking.
- **Evidence bonds.** A separate Verifier re-derives each claim from *only* its cited crop, with
  everything else blacked out, then swaps the crop for a random same-size region. If the finding
  survives the swap, it was never using its evidence, and the claim is struck, even if it happened to
  be right.
- **Self-calibration.** Every threshold is measured from documents the deployment certifies as
  authentic. Nothing ships as a constant.

No single agent can produce a claim, its justification and its validation.

## The bug that shaped it

The first version called every image tampered. Run against 13 real, unmodified receipts and camera
photos, it returned 13/13 `tamper_detected`: a 100% false-positive rate. The rebuild traced six root
causes, among them thresholds that were never measured against anything, forensic probes that existed
but were never called, evidence leaking between images in a batch, and bond tests that could not fail.

## Measured

On a 134-image corpus (29 authentic, 105 forged across 5 forgery modes), with calibration and
evaluation sources kept disjoint:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| False-accusation rate | **3.4%** (1/29 authentic), from 100% |
| Precision | **0.947** |
| Recall, overall | **17.1%** (18/105) |
| Recall, retyped amount | **52.4%** |

**The limits, as the repository states them.** Most forgeries still get through. Pixel-conservative
ones are mostly out of reach: a single spliced digit is caught 4.8% of the time, an in-place
re-compression 9.5%. The language layer in the original
plan is not built; the deciding agent reasons over calibrated statistics directly, with no LLM call.

The banner is the repository's own test pair: an authentic scanned receipt and the same receipt with
one line retyped, the edited region outlined. They are corpus samples, not a field case.
